About Me

CV

Hello, I'm Aswin R Nair! I am currently a Research Assistant at the Raman Research Institute in Bengaluru. I create simulations for radiative transport in the Interstellar Medium. I graduated with a bachelor's in physics and master's degree in astrophysics from the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research, Mohali in 2025.

In the past I have worked on various numerical simulations that deal with diverse astrophysical phenomena, where I use both analytical (equations and stuff) and computational methods (simulations and stuff) to model and understand these systems. The latest of which is my masters thesis. It was on "Numerical Simulation of Accretion onto Compact Objects with Hard Surface" and you can read more about it here.

Right now I am trying to transition into the field of Technical AI safety. Although my primary academic interest was astrophysics in the past, the tremendous potential of AI has impressed me. I personally susbscribe to the idea that AI can bring about a utopia, a golden age where solving the problems of the world is no longer constrained by capability. However possessing this level of capability also poses serious risks. Traditionally powerful technology often falls into misuse but we also run into a problem that is unique to the technology - misalignment. Preventing AI from exposing humanity to grave danger through misuse and misalignment is an existential problem, and I feel like contributing to such an impactful problem is a fulfilling use of my time and energy. Governance and policy changes paired with tehcnical safety efforts is how we are currently attacking this problem, I feel like my experiences in computational astrophysics will help me contribute through the technical front.

I was part of the BlueDot Impact Technical AI safety May 2026 cohort. I am working on a personal project that involves understanding what we can learn from activation directions extracted from different evaluation datasets. My latest work involved investigating "Can Models learn to hide activations, without being taught to" as part of the BlueDot Impact Technical AI safety project.
