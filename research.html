Can a Model Hide Without Being Taught To?

Substack GitHub

Large Language Models are capable of reasoning about their tasks internally, i.e., they are capable of thinking about and executing tasks while the trace of their thoughts might not be visible in their outputs or even in their Chain-of-Thought outputs. This necissitates the monitoring of their activations - the numbers that stream through the brains of an LLM when it is computing its task, akin to directly peering into its brain as thoughts form. Can a model learn to hide even these activations, without any finetuning or RL, entirely in context?

Investigating the direction of Truth

Github

It has been shown that refusal - the behaviour in large language models to refuse answers to harmful prompts - is mediated by a single direction in its activation space. So what other such concepts are represented in such a simple geometry? Creativity? Helpfulness? Honesty? This project attempts to extract such a direction in its activation space that corresponds to "correctness" - the concept that allows it to answer questions correctly. However, the direction that is obtained depends heavily on what the model is being correct about, i.e , the datasets used for obtaining this direction heavily influences what the model is being honest about.

Numerical Simulation of Accretion onto Compact Objects with Hard Surface

Thesis PDF Poster

Extended black hole solutions to include the effects of a hard surface on accretion processes, thus enabling the simulation of accretion onto a neutron star/white dwarf surface. The simulation was made in Fortran, C++ and Python.
